Pike push-up

Raise hips and bend elbows to lower the head slightly in front of the hands; press back up with control.

Push patternIntermediate · relative difficultyDetailed technique & anatomy

Movement illustration

What works?

Primary emphasis: Shoulders, triceps

Important assistance: Upper chest, serratus anterior, trunk

Mint = primary region · Amber = assisting region. These are simplified anatomical regions, not measurements of muscle activity. Body position changes the emphasis.

How to perform it

  1. Start in a pike position, hips high and hands planted on a nonslip floor.
  2. Shift a manageable share of weight into the hands.
  3. Bend the arms to lower the head toward a point slightly ahead of the hands.
  4. Press the floor away to return to the pike without a rushed neck movement.

Breathe continuously and use a range that feels controlled and comfortable for your body.

Why this movement matters

Pressing movements generally challenge the chest, shoulder and elbow extensor muscles. Which muscles contribute most changes with leverage, hand placement, range of motion and individual technique.

Progression checkpoint

Progress when shoulder comfort and balance are repeatable.

Equipment and safety

Reduce pressure on painful wrists or shoulders by changing the angle, hand position or variation.

Problem solving

Common mistakes & corrections

Head descends straight between the hands with neck strain

Correction: Use a smaller range and position your gaze between hands.

Hips drop and movement becomes a floor push-up

Correction: Maintain a pike shape or regress to incline pressing.

Where this fits in a program

Pike push-up is a push exercise. Pair it with movements from other patterns over the training week, and choose sets or hold times you can recover from. See the Balanced full-body program for adaptable examples; there is no universally correct repetition target.
