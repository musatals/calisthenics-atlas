Incline push-up

Keep hands on a stable elevated surface; lower your chest toward it while maintaining torso alignment.

What works?

Primary emphasis: Chest, triceps

Important assistance: Front shoulders, serratus anterior, trunk

Mint = primary region · Amber = assisting region. These are simplified anatomical regions, not measurements of muscle activity. Body position changes the emphasis.

How to perform it

  1. Choose a firm counter or securely anchored high surface.
  2. Place hands on the edge and step back until body is straight.
  3. Bend elbows, bringing the chest toward the surface without collapsing through the hips.
  4. Press away and exhale without bouncing.

Breathe continuously and use a range that feels controlled and comfortable for your body.

Why this movement matters

Pressing movements generally challenge the chest, shoulder and elbow extensor muscles. Which muscles contribute most changes with leverage, hand placement, range of motion and individual technique.

Progression checkpoint

Lower the incline only after smooth, repeatable sets.

Equipment and safety

Reduce pressure on painful wrists or shoulders by changing the angle, hand position or variation.

Problem solving

Common mistakes & corrections

The surface shifts or slides

Correction: Use an immovable surface and test it before putting weight on it.

Hips sag during the descent

Correction: Raise the incline, shorten the set and gently brace the abdomen.

Where this fits in a program

Incline push-up is a push exercise. Pair it with movements from other patterns over the training week, and choose sets or hold times you can recover from. See the Foundation program for adaptable examples; there is no universally correct repetition target.
