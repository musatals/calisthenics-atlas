Glute bridge

Press through planted feet and raise hips using glutes, avoiding excessive lower-back arch.

What works?

Primary emphasis: Gluteals

Important assistance: Hamstrings, trunk

Mint = primary region · Amber = assisting region. These are simplified anatomical regions, not measurements of muscle activity. Body position changes the emphasis.

How to perform it

  1. Lie on your back with knees bent and feet on the floor.
  2. Position feet where you can press evenly through them.
  3. Raise the hips by squeezing the glutes, keeping the ribs controlled.
  4. Lower slowly without dropping the pelvis.

Breathe continuously and use a range that feels controlled and comfortable for your body.

Why this movement matters

Lower-body patterns share work across hip, knee and ankle musculature. Joint angles, depth, support and individual body proportions change the emphasis.

Progression checkpoint

Progress once pelvis remains level and the movement is comfortable.

Equipment and safety

Use a support and comfortable depth when balance, knee or ankle tolerance is a limiting factor.

Problem solving

Common mistakes & corrections

Lower back does most of the movement

Correction: Reduce height and think of extending at the hips rather than arching.

Feet are so far away that hamstrings cramp

Correction: Move feet a little closer and reduce the hold.
