Scapular pull-up

From a supported hang, move shoulder blades slightly without bending elbows substantially.

Pull patternBeginner · relative difficultyDetailed technique & anatomy

Movement illustration

What works?

Primary emphasis: Lower trapezius, latissimus dorsi

Important assistance: Forearms, serratus anterior, upper back

Mint = primary region · Amber = assisting region. These are simplified anatomical regions, not measurements of muscle activity. Body position changes the emphasis.

How to perform it

  1. Grip a secure bar with feet supported when needed.
  2. Keep elbows mostly straight while initiating movement from shoulder blades.
  3. Move body a small distance by drawing shoulders down and toward stability.
  4. Relax back to starting position without dropping abruptly.

Breathe continuously and use a range that feels controlled and comfortable for your body.

Why this movement matters

Pulling movements involve the back and elbow flexors working together with the shoulder girdle. Grip, body angle and assistance change the relative contribution of these regions.

Progression checkpoint

Build repeatable small-range scapular control before more demanding hangs.

Equipment and safety

Only use verified load-bearing equipment; accidental falls and unstable anchors are avoidable hazards.

Problem solving

Common mistakes & corrections

Large arm bend turns the drill into a partial pull-up

Correction: Use less effort and think small shoulder movement.

Shoulders are pushed painfully into end range

Correction: Shorten range and add foot support.

Where this fits in a program

Scapular pull-up is a pull exercise. Pair it with movements from other patterns over the training week, and choose sets or hold times you can recover from. See the Foundation program for adaptable examples; there is no universally correct repetition target.
