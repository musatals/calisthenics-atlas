Bird dog

On hands and knees, extend opposite limbs without rotating the trunk.

Core patternBeginner · relative difficultyDetailed technique & anatomy

Movement illustration

← Browse exercisesUse in a training plan →

What works?

Primary emphasis: Spinal extensors, abdominal stabilizers

Important assistance: Gluteals, shoulders

Mint = primary region · Amber = assisting region. These are simplified anatomical regions, not measurements of muscle activity. Body position changes the emphasis.

How to perform it

  1. Start on hands and knees on a comfortable surface.
  2. Brace gently while keeping pelvis roughly level.
  3. Extend opposite arm and leg without rotating the torso.
  4. Return and alternate sides with a brief controlled pause.

Breathe continuously and use a range that feels controlled and comfortable for your body.

Why this movement matters

Trunk exercises build a capacity to control unwanted movement while breathing. A visible abdominal contraction is not a direct measure of spinal loading or exercise quality.

Progression checkpoint

Increase reach or hold length only after the trunk remains steady.

Equipment and safety

Continue breathing; shorten holds or leverage if abdominal control fades or discomfort increases.

Problem solving

Common mistakes & corrections

Leg raises too high, arching the lower back

Correction: Reach long rather than high.

Pelvis rotates during the reach

Correction: Shorten the extension and move slowly.

Where this fits in a program

Bird dog is a core exercise. Pair it with movements from other patterns over the training week, and choose sets or hold times you can recover from. See the Foundation program for adaptable examples; there is no universally correct repetition target.
