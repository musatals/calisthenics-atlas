Programming

Progressive Overload Without Weights

Progressive overload means making training sufficiently challenging over time as your capacity improves.

Adjust repetition volume

Add repetitions or sets within your ability while preserving technique and recovery.

Change leverage

An incline push-up is often easier than a floor push-up; a decline push-up is often harder. Similar adjustments apply to many bodyweight skills.

Use a simple log

Track the variation, repetitions, sets, rest and effort. Not every session must be harder than the last; trends matter more than isolated workouts.

More than repetitions

Challenge can grow through longer range, lower assistance, changed leverage, more controlled time under tension and additional working sets.

Keep one variable in focus

For example, move from a high incline push-up to a slightly lower incline while keeping set and repetition goals manageable.

Leave room to recover

Avoid increasing difficulty, session count and total sets all at once. Repeatable training quality is an important measure of success.

Educational guidance only. Adapt exercise to your abilities and seek professional advice when needed.
