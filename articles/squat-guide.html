Bodyweight Squats and Lower-Body Strength

Bodyweight squats train coordinated hip, knee and ankle movement and can be adapted to many ability levels.

Find your stance

Stand with feet at a comfortable width and let knees follow the general direction of the toes.

Control your range

Sit down and back between the legs as mobility permits, keeping balance over the feet. There is no universal squat depth for everyone.

Make it accessible

Use a chair for support or reduce depth if necessary. Progress to split squats, lunges or single-leg variations when appropriate.

Fit the stance to your body

Foot placement and depth vary with anatomy, mobility and task. Keep the movement comfortable and controlled rather than forcing a universal stance.

Useful variations

Chair squats offer a clear target; split squats, step-ups and reverse lunges build unilateral control. A supported single-leg squat is an optional later variation.

Progress with purpose

Develop a consistent range and stable balance before choosing harder single-leg movements.

Educational guidance only. Adapt exercise to your abilities and seek professional advice when needed.
