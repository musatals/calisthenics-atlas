Warm-Ups and Movement Preparation

Prepare for your sessions with practical warm-ups.

Why warm up?

A warm-up helps you transition toward harder activity and rehearse the movements you plan to perform. Its goal is readiness, not exhaustion.

A practical 5–10 minute sequence

Begin with two to five minutes of comfortable walking or marching. Follow with easy shoulder, hip and ankle movements, then one or two lighter sets of your first exercise.

Make it task-specific

Before push-ups, practice easier incline push-ups. Before squats, perform comfortable unweighted squats. Before pulling, use light assisted rows or supported hangs.

Keep it flexible

You do not need a long or identical routine every day. Adjust preparation to your session, comfort and environment.

General educational guidance only. Exercise within comfortable limits, and seek individualized professional advice when appropriate.
